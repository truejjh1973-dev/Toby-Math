Algebra

Expressions, equations, quadratics, and functions — gathered across contests, courses, and formulas.

Why algebra matters more than anything

Every other branch of high-school math is algebra wearing a costume. Geometry is algebra about lengths and angles, trigonometry is algebra about ratios, calculus is algebra about rates. If the algebra below feels automatic, everything else becomes easier.

The three identities to know cold

Expanding and factoring are two views of the same three patterns. Master these and the rest follows:

$$(a + b)^2 = a^2 + 2ab + b^2$$ $$(a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2$$ $$(a - b)(a + b) = a^2 - b^2$$

The quadratic formula

For $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$, with $a \neq 0$, the solutions are

$$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$

The quantity $b^2 - 4ac$ — the discriminant — tells you what to expect before you finish:

Value of $b^2 - 4ac$Nature of solutions
$> 0$Two distinct real solutions
$= 0$One repeated real solution
$< 0$No real solutions (two complex)
In contests: discriminant questions love to hide in number theory. A problem about "real roots for all values of $k$" almost always reduces to "the discriminant is never positive".

Check your algebra

1. Expand $(x + 3)^2$.

Middle term: $2 \cdot x \cdot 3 = 6x$, so $(x+3)^2 = x^2 + 6x + 9$.

2. Which is the correct factorization of $x^2 - 9$?

Difference of squares: $x^2 - 9 = x^2 - 3^2 = (x - 3)(x + 3)$.

3. For $x^2 + 4x + 5 = 0$, the discriminant $b^2 - 4ac$ is…

$4^2 - 4(1)(5) = 16 - 20 = -4$ — negative, so there are no real solutions.
