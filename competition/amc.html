Getting started with the AMC

Format, scoring, and the fastest way to improve your first attempt.

What the AMC is

The American Mathematics Competitions (AMC 8, AMC 10, and AMC 12) are the entry point of the competition ladder in North America. They are multiple choice, so a fast, careful reasoner is rewarded every bit as much as a fast calculator.

A high AMC score invites you to the AIME — the next rung of the ladder, where answers are integers and guessing stops working.

AMC 8 scoring: one point per correct answer

On the AMC 8, each correct answer is worth 1 point; incorrect and unanswered questions are worth 0. There is no penalty for guessing, and the maximum score is 25. Do not apply the AMC 10/12 blank-answer bonus below to the AMC 8.

Getting to the AIME

ContestFormatAnswers
AIME I & II15 problems, 3 hoursInteger answers, 000–999

Scoring is 1 point per correct answer with no penalty — the risk is time, not guessing. My own path: AMC 10A 111 → AMC 10B 123 → AIME II 10/15. The pattern that worked: bank the first ten problems fast, then spend the saved time carefully on 11–15.

  • There is no penalty for guessing on the AIME — blank and wrong both score 0.
  • Answers are always integers from 000 to 999 — no fractions, no radicals.
  • The AIME feeds the USAMO ladder and, for Canadians, makes a strong portfolio line.

AMC 10/12 scoring: answer early, guess wisely

On the AMC 10 and 12, a correct answer is worth 6 points, a blank is worth 1.5, and a wrong answer is worth 0. That single rule should shape your strategy:

  • A wrong answer costs you 1.5 points vs. leaving it blank — so never guess blindly.
  • On AMC 10/12, only guess when you can eliminate options; otherwise, skip.

How to prepare

  1. Do past papers under real time. Practice with the clock, not just with the questions.
  2. Learn to draw. Contest geometry rewards a good sketch before algebra.
  3. Keep a mistake log. The fastest improvement comes from patterns, not puzzles.
"The AMC rewards the competitor who keeps calm on problem 23, not just the one who nails problem 1–10."
Quick win: the last five questions (21–25) are where the difficulty lives. Score the first 20 solidly before hunting for extra marks at the end.
