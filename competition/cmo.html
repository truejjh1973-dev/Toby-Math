CMO — Canadian Mathematical Olympiad

Canada's national olympiad: invitation-only, proof-based, and the start of the international ladder.

What the CMO is

The Canadian Mathematical Olympiad is the highest general-mathematics contest in Canada, run by the Canadian Mathematical Society. It is invitation-only: most writers qualify through a strong COMC result. It is not a race against a clock the way school tests are — it is a test of calm, careful written reasoning.

FormatDetails
Length3 hours
Problems5 full-solution problems, 7 marks each
StyleProof-based — no calculators, no multiple choice
EligibilityBy invitation, typically from the COMC results

Why it matters

  • A CMO seat is a national-level credential on any application.
  • It feeds the selection process toward Canada's team for the IMO.
  • The problems teach the kind of thinking the first years of an undergraduate proof course demand.

How to prepare

  1. Build the habits first. Full written solutions on earlier rounds — Euclid, COMC Part B — are the training ground.
  2. Practise past CMO problems. Their structure repeats: a lemma, a case split, a clean finish.
  3. Write for a reader. Assume the marker knows no shortcuts: justify everything, elegantly.
"On the CMO, quiet persistence beats flashy insight every time."
Official site: cms.math.ca/competitions/cmo — recent problems, results, and the invitation criteria.
