Problem-solving strategy

Four habits that turn a "no idea" problem into a "where do I start" problem.

1. Restate the problem in your own words

Contest wording is often the hardest part. Strip every number, write the situation as a tiny story, and label everything with a letter. Most "hard" questions are one restatement away from being clear.

2. Try a special case first

When the general problem feels heavy, shrink it. Replace the big number with something small (try n = 1, 2, 3), a short list instead of a long one, a square instead of a general polygon. The pattern you find in the small case is usually the pattern that scales.

3. Work backwards

Ask what the final answer has to look like. In a full-solution contest, decide which facts you'd most like to be true, then check whether the problem lets you prove them. Reverse engineering often exposes the intended road.

4. Guess, check, and keep a log

An educated guess gives you a target to verify — and the verification is where the real reading of the problem happens. Record every problem you couldn't finish and the single idea that would have fixed it. That log is the fastest textbook you will ever own.

"You don't need to win with the first move; you need to make a move that keeps the game alive."
In practice: out of three minutes on a stubborn problem, spend the first one finding a smaller version you can solve. Momentum beats staring.
